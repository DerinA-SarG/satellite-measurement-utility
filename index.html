Roof pitch & unit rate

Pitch converts flat footprint to sloped roof surface. Warehouses are usually 0.25–0.5:12, which adds well under 1%.

Satellite imagery

Without a key the map uses Esri World Imagery, which is free and needs no account. A key switches to Google’s Map Tiles API, which is sharper in some areas and billed per tile by Google. If Google fails for any reason the map falls back to Esri on its own.

Measuring accurately

  • Trace the roof, not the walls. Satellite imagery is rarely straight overhead, so a tall building leans. Its roof outline sits offset from its footprint — pick one and stay consistent. For roof area, trace the roof edge all the way around.
  • Zoom in before the first click. At zoom 20 each pixel is roughly 6 in, so a careless corner costs a few feet.
  • Hold Shift while drawing to snap each new wall square to the previous one. Most warehouses are rectangular, and this is faster and more accurate than eyeballing the corners.
  • Imagery dates vary. A recent expansion may not be visible. Cross-check against the county parcel viewer if the number matters.
  • Areas use a local ellipsoidal projection anchored at each shape, so they are accurate to well under 0.01% at building scale — tighter than the spherical math most web tools use.

The tools

  • Area — click each corner of the roof, then double-click or press Enter to close it.
  • Line — click along a route for a distance. Each segment is measured as well as the total.
  • Offset — select a shape, set a distance, and get a new shape covering the ground that distance outside it. It is only the added strip: the shape you offset is left alone and is not counted twice. All the way round that strip is a ring with the original left out of the middle of it; along a few sides it is a band, and sides on opposite walls come out as separate shapes. Useful for fire lanes, setbacks and laydown yards. It works on lines too, which gives you a corridor of a given width.

Editing

Drag a corner handle to move it. Click a hollow midpoint handle to add a corner. Right-click a corner to remove it. Drag the blue grip at the centre of a selected shape to slide the whole thing around. Ctrl+Z takes back the last change — a move, a new shape, a deletion, even Clear.

Picking sides

With an area selected, Numbers puts each side’s number on the map, at the middle of that side. Click a number, or the matching chip in the sidebar, to take that side in or out of the offset. They stay up until you switch them off again.

Colour

Each shape has a colour chip beside its name in the list. Click it to pick any colour; right-click it to go back to the default. Colours are saved with your work and carried into exported .kml and .geojson files.
